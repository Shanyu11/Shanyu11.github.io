Two Stage Rocket

Launched a 9.4-foot two-stage rocket. Stages separate in flight via a Marman clamp mechanism, and the vehicle surpassed its simulated apogee prediction by 1.8× on its first flight.

Timeline: Oct 2025 to Oct 2026 Role: Design and fabrication, airframe team Team: UCSD Rocket Propulsion Laboratory Tools: OpenRocket · SolidWorks · Blueraven altimeter

Flight, October 17, 2026

The rocket flew on October 17, 2026 and reached an apogee of 1877 ft, 1.8 times the altitude predicted by the pre-flight simulations. The stages separated as planned, and all sections were recovered.

Apogee

Two stage design

The two stages are held together by a Marman clamp, a split-ring band fastener that releases under a pyrotechnic charge. The stages separate in flight without a large lateral impulse to either section.

The Marman clamp keeps both stages stacked as one assembly on the pad and separates them at altitude. Compared with shear pins or rail-based separation, the band clamp is more compact and repeatable. The interface between the stages is rigid enough to survive ignition loads and still releases on command.
