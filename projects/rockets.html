Staged Rockets

Designing and flying solid-propellant rockets with UCSD Rocket Propulsion Laboratory — including a two-stage demonstrator with a custom staging mechanism, built as the prototype for a 15 ft Spaceport America Cup vehicle.

Role: Rocket engineer — staging mechanism Timeline: Oct 2025 – present Status: Flown, separated, fully recovered Tools: OpenRocket · MATLAB · SolidWorks

Objective

Multi-stage rockets fail most often at the staging event, so before the team commits to a 15 ft rocket-candy vehicle (35% sorbitol / 65% potassium nitrate) for the Spaceport America Cup, we built a scaled demonstrator to prove the staging mechanism in flight.

Result The two-stage demonstrator achieved clean stage separation in flight with full recovery of both stages and avionics, using a custom staging system designed with feedback from SpaceX engineers.

The staging mechanism

I owned the staging mechanism: the retention of the upper stage during boost, the actuation that releases it, and the release sequence that keeps both stages stable through separation. Design details and flight footage are held at the team's request while the Spaceport America Cup vehicle is in development — I am glad to walk through the mechanism in an interview.

G-class vehicle

900 ft/s

Separately: designed, simulated, and programmed a 24.21 in long, 1.6 in diameter G-class rocket, launched at the Friends of Amateur Rocketry facility. Simulated in OpenRocket and MATLAB; reached 900 ft/s with successful recovery of the rocket and avionics.
