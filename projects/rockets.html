Two Stage Rocket

A 9.4 ft two stage rocket built with the UC San Diego Rocket Propulsion Laboratory. The stages separate in flight with a Marman clamp. On its first flight it reached 1877 ft, 1.8 times the simulated apogee.

Timeline: Oct 2025 to Oct 2026 Tools: OpenRocket (flight simulation) · SolidWorks · Blueraven altimeter

Flight

Staging mechanism

Description

A two stage rocket drops its first stage once that motor burns out, then lights a second motor and keeps climbing. The handoff, called staging, is where multi stage rockets most often fail. The club is building a 15 ft two stage rocket for the Spaceport America Cup, the largest collegiate rocketry competition. Before committing to the full size rocket, the team built this 9.4 ft demonstrator to prove the staging mechanism in flight.

I worked on that mechanism: a Marman clamp, a steel band with teeth that holds the two stages together during the first burn and releases them on command. SpaceX engineers gave pointers on the design. The rocket flew on October 17, 2026. It reached 1877 ft, 1.8 times the apogee predicted in simulation, the stages separated cleanly, and both stages and the flight electronics were recovered.
