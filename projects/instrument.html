Drug Discovery
Optimization Machine

A machine that automates a routine lab task in one device. Today that task needs several pieces of equipment and a person moving samples between them. I built the instrument alone, from interviews with scientists to a working prototype. What the machine does is confidential until the patent is filed.

Timeline: Jun 2026 to present Tools: ESP32 microcontroller · C++ · SolidWorks · 3D printing

Demo

Description

During my internship at Bayer / Vividion Therapeutics, a pharmaceutical research company, I interviewed a scientist and the lab engineers about a routine task they repeat many times a day. It runs across several machines, and a person handles the sample between every step. I turned the interviews into requirements for workflow fit, cost, and buildability, then designed and built a single device that runs the whole task in place. Bench tests matched the standalone equipment it replaces. Based on the interviews, it saves a scientist 5+ hours a week.

Technical

  • Control: C++ firmware on an ESP32 microcontroller that sequences the full cycle.
  • Electronics: custom driver circuits for the moving parts.
  • Mechanical: 3D printed enclosure modeled in SolidWorks, three revisions.

Firmware and design documents stay private until the patent is filed.
