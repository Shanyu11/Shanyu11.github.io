BEED

A benchtop lab-automation instrument that consolidates a repetitive multi-machine sample-prep workflow into a single device — a self-directed product build from user research to bench-validated prototype. Design specifics are confidential while a patent filing is prepared.

Role: everything — research, design, firmware, electronics, validation Timeline: Jun 2026 – present Status: Rev 3, bench-validated Stack: ESP32 · C++ · SolidWorks · 3D printing IP: details confidential · patent filing in preparation

Demo

Rev 3 on the bench

The problem

Preparing a sample in a formulation lab is a multi-instrument workflow: successive prep steps happen on separate machines on different parts of the bench, so scientists physically carry samples back and forth, re-securing and re-monitoring at each step — for every sample, all day.

During my internship at Bayer / Vividion Therapeutics, user-discovery interviews with a formulation scientist and lab engineers surfaced how much time this eats. I translated those interviews into design requirements around workflow fit, cost, and buildability — then built the machine.

Objective One device that performs the full prep cycle in place. Projected impact from interviews: eliminates manual machine-swapping and saves a scientist 5+ hours per week of sample-prep time.

Process

3 revisions

Each revision changed something I got wrong the time before. That's the point of showing them.

Technical

  • Control: ESP32 firmware written in C++ that sequences the full prep cycle end to end.
  • Electronics: custom breadboarded driver circuits for the actuation subsystems — circuit specifics to follow after the patent filing.
  • Mechanical: 3D-printed enclosure modeled in SolidWorks, iterated across three revisions.
  • Feasibility: iterated trade-offs between component cost, thermal management, and driver power across revisions.

Firmware and detailed design documentation are private while the patent filing is prepared — happy to walk through both in interviews.

Validation & next

Bench validation covered the performance of both core functions against the standalone equipment the device replaces. Next: continued duty-cycle testing and pilot use inside a real formulation workflow.
